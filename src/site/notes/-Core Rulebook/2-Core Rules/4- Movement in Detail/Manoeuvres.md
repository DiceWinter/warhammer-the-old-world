###### Manoevres
There are six types of manoeuvre to choose from: [[-Core Rulebook/2-Core Rules/4- Movement in Detail/Manoeuvres#1. Wheel\|wheel]], [[-Core Rulebook/2-Core Rules/4- Movement in Detail/Manoeuvres#2. Turn\|turn]], [[-Core Rulebook/2-Core Rules/4- Movement in Detail/Manoeuvres#3. Move Backwards\|move backwards]], [[-Core Rulebook/2-Core Rules/4- Movement in Detail/Manoeuvres#4. Move Sideways\|move sideways]], [[-Core Rulebook/2-Core Rules/4- Movement in Detail/Manoeuvres#5. Redress The Ranks\|redress the ranks]] and [[-Core Rulebook/2-Core Rules/4- Movement in Detail/Manoeuvres#6. Reform\|reform]]. During its movement, a unit may perform a single manoeuvre. Regardless of the manoeuvre performed, no model can move more than twice its [[-Core Rulebook/1-Overview of the Game/2-Model Profiles/Model Profiles#Movement (M)\|Movement]] characteristic.

---
#### 1. Wheel
‘Wheeling’ is the best way of making an alteration to the direction a unit is facing whilst still being able to advance. To wheel, the leading edge of the unit moves forward, pivoting
round one of its front corners, as shown in [Fig 124.1](/img/user/Images/Diagrams/Manoeuvres%20Fig%20124.1.png).

When a unit wheels, every model counts as having moved as far as the outside model. Once the wheel is complete, you may use any movement the unit has remaining. Unless it is [[-Core Rulebook/2-Core Rules/4- Movement in Detail/The Charge Move\|charging]], a unit can wheel more than once during its move and can alternate between moving forward and wheeling.

---
#### 2. Turn
To execute a turn, all the models remain in place but are turned through 90° or 180° to face their side or rear. For every 90° it turns, a unit uses a quarter of its [[-Core Rulebook/1-Overview of the Game/2-Model Profiles/Model Profiles#Movement (M)\|Movement]] characteristic. ([See Fig 124.2, 124.3 and 124.4](/img/user/Images/Diagrams/Manoeuvres%20Fig%20124.2%20124.3%20124.4.png))

When a unit turns, all models in complete ranks simply turn on the spot. Any models in an incomplete rear rank are moved to the rear of the new formation.

When a unit is turned to face its side or rear, any Command models it contains (see [[-Core Rulebook/3-Advanced Rules/4-Command Groups/Command Groups\|Command Groups]]) are automatically placed into the new front rank. If there is not enough space in the front rank, such models are placed in the second rank.

---
#### 3. Move Backwards
Units can move backwards as well as forward, but tend to shuffle carefully rather than stride purposefully. To represent this, a unit that moves backwards must halve its [[-Core Rulebook/1-Overview of the Game/2-Model Profiles/Model Profiles#Movement (M)\|Movement]] characteristic.

---
#### 4. Move Sideways
Units can move sideways as well as forward, but do so with caution in order to maintain their formation. To represent this, a unit that moves sideways must halve its [[-Core Rulebook/1-Overview of the Game/2-Model Profiles/Model Profiles#Movement (M)\|Movement]] characteristic.

---
#### 5. Redress The Ranks
Units can redress the ranks by moving models to or from their rear ranks to decrease or increase the number of models in their front rank. A unit may use half of its [[-Core Rulebook/1-Overview of the Game/2-Model Profiles/Model Profiles#Movement (M)\|Movement]] characteristic in order to deduct up to five models from its front rank (as shown in [Fig 125.1](/img/user/Images/Diagrams/Manoeuvres%20Fig%20125.1%20125.2.png)), or to add up to five models to its front rank (as shown in [Fig 125.2](/img/user/Images/Diagrams/Manoeuvres%20Fig%20125.1%20125.2.png)).

The models in the remaining ranks are then rearranged to match the number of models in the front rank and maintain a correct formation. Remember, there must be the same number of models in each rank. Only the rear rank may have fewer models.

---
#### 6. Reform
A reform represents the most complex of manoeuvres a unit can perform on the battlefield, but on the tabletop it is quite straightforward. Reforming allows a unit to sacrifice its entire movement in order to both pivot about its centre to change its facing by up to 180°, and to rearrange its ranks and files as required to either change its formation, or to adopt a different formation.

---
#### The Ends Of The World
It is important to note that the edge of the battlefield does not represent the end of the world. It is perfectly acceptable for a corner of a unit to cross beyond the edge of the battlefield during a manoeuvre, provided the unit is able to end its movement completely upon the battlefield.
