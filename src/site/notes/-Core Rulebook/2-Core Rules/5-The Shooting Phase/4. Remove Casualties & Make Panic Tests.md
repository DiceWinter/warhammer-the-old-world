###### 4. Remove Casualties & Make Panic Tests
Unsaved wounds are applied to the target unit, causing models to be removed as casualties. If a unit loses enough models, it will have to take a [[-Core Rulebook/2-Core Rules/7-The Psychology of War/Panic Tests\|‘Panic’ test]] and may fall back or flee.

---
#### Remove Casualties
Each unsaved wound is applied to the target unit, each causing one [[-Core Rulebook/1-Overview of the Game/2-Model Profiles/Model Profiles#Wounds (W)\|Wound]] to be lost, as described on [[-Core Rulebook/1-Overview of the Game/4-Removing Casualties/Removing Casualties\|Removing Casualties]]. When a model is reduced to zero Wounds, it becomes a casualty and is removed from play. This continues until there are no more unsaved wounds to be applied to the unit, or there are no models remaining to be removed as casualties.

---
#### Make Panic Tests
Rarely does shooting alone destroy an army, but it can thin the enemy’s numbers considerably, causing regiments to lose their nerve. When they do, they will often fall back and, sometimes, turn tail and flee.

If, during a single [[-Core Rulebook/2-Core Rules/5-The Shooting Phase/The Shooting Phase Sequence\|Shooting phase]], a unit loses more than a quarter (25%) of the models it contained at the start of that Shooting phase, it must immediately make a [[-Core Rulebook/2-Core Rules/7-The Psychology of War/Panic Tests\|‘Panic’ test]]. To make a Panic test, test against the unit’s [[-Core Rulebook/1-Overview of the Game/2-Model Profiles/Model Profiles#Leadership (Ld)\|Leadership]] characteristic. If this test is failed, the unit has succumbed to panic. If this test is passed, the unit remains resolute and does not panic.

---
#### Fall Back Or Flee
What happens to a unit that fails a Panic test will depend upon how many casualties it has suffered. A unit that has suffered only a few casualties will [[-Core Rulebook/2-Core Rules/4- Movement in Detail/Give Ground & Fall Back In Good Order#Fall Back In Good Order\|Fall Back in Good Order]], whereas a unit that has suffered a significant amount of casualties will turn tail and [[-Core Rulebook/2-Core Rules/4- Movement in Detail/Flee!\|flee]].

**Fall Back in Good Order:** If a unit still contains more than half (50%) of the models it contained at the start of the battle, it will [[-Core Rulebook/2-Core Rules/4- Movement in Detail/Give Ground & Fall Back In Good Order#Fall Back In Good Order\|Fall Back in Good Order]]. The unit moves directly away from the enemy unit whose shooting caused it to make the [[-Core Rulebook/2-Core Rules/7-The Psychology of War/Panic Tests\|Panic test]].

**Flee:** If a unit contains only half (50%), or fewer than half, of the models it contained at the start of the battle, it will immediately turn tail and flee. The unit flees directly away from the enemy unit whose shooting caused it to make the Panic test.

---
#### No Need For Hysterics
A unit is only required to make a single [[-Core Rulebook/2-Core Rules/7-The Psychology of War/Panic Tests\|Panic test]] during any [[-Core Rulebook/2-Core Rules/5-The Shooting Phase/The Shooting Phase Sequence\|Shooting phase]]. For example, if a unit loses more than a quarter of its number to shooting from one enemy unit but passes its Panic test, it will not have to take another Panic test during that same Shooting phase even if it then suffers more casualties from another enemy unit. This is because, whilst shooting is resolved one unit at a time, in reality all units would shoot more or less simultaneously. Therefore, all casualties would fall at roughly the same time.
