###### Terrain & Combat
Terrain is covered in more detail in the [[-Core Rulebook/4-Battlefield Terrain/How Much Terrain？\|Battlefield Terrain]] section. This page explains how certain types of terrain affect combat. Players should discuss before the battle begins which category a terrain feature falls into.
#### [[-Core Rulebook/4-Battlefield Terrain/Categories Of Terrain#Open Ground\|Open Ground]] & [[-Core Rulebook/4-Battlefield Terrain/Categories Of Terrain#Hills\|Hills]]
Open ground is the ideal setting for combat. However, care should be taken to consider the role of hills when calculating combat results. Remember, units that occupy the high ground can claim a bonus.
#### [[-Core Rulebook/4-Battlefield Terrain/Categories Of Terrain#Difficult Terrain\|Difficult Terrain]]
If a quarter (25%) or more of the models within a unit are within difficult terrain at the start of the [[-Core Rulebook/2-Core Rules/6-The Combat Phase/The Combat Phase Sequence\|Combat phase]], it becomes [[-Core Rulebook/1-Overview of the Game/3-Forming Units/Disrupted Units\|Disrupted]] and cannot claim a Rank Bonus.
##### Dangerous Terrain & Woods
For the purposes of combat, both [[-Core Rulebook/4-Battlefield Terrain/Categories Of Terrain#Dangerous Terrain\|dangerous]] terrain and [[-Core Rulebook/4-Battlefield Terrain/Categories Of Terrain#Woods\|woods]] are treated as difficult terrain.
#### [[-Core Rulebook/4-Battlefield Terrain/Categories Of Terrain#Impassable Terrain\|Impassable Terrain]]
Should a charging unit ever be unable to align to the enemy due to the presence of [[-Core Rulebook/4-Battlefield Terrain/Categories Of Terrain#Impassable Terrain\|impassable]] terrain, the charging unit has made a [[-Core Rulebook/2-Core Rules/6-The Combat Phase/1.1. Choose Combat & Determine Who Can Fight#^6657e9\|disordered charge]]. In addition, should it prove impossible to align the units once contact has been made, causing a gap to be left, the charging unit becomes [[-Core Rulebook/1-Overview of the Game/3-Forming Units/Disrupted Units\|Disrupted]].
#### Linear Obstacles
Linear obstacles can have a considerable impact upon combat, depending upon their height.

**[[-Core Rulebook/4-Battlefield Terrain/Categories Of Terrain#^066141\|Low Linear Obstacles]]:** Should a unit engaged in combat ever find itself straddling a low linear obstacle (i.e., less than 2" high), such as walls and hedges, it becomes [[-Core Rulebook/1-Overview of the Game/3-Forming Units/Disrupted Units\|Disrupted]].

**[[-Core Rulebook/4-Battlefield Terrain/Categories Of Terrain#^20e81e\|Defended Low Linear Obstacles]]:** A unit behind a low linear obstacle can defend it by moving its front rank into base contact with the obstacle. Enemies can [[-Core Rulebook/2-Core Rules/4- Movement in Detail/The Charge Move\|charge]] the defenders as normal but do not have to physically cross the obstacle. Instead, the front rank of a charging unit moves into base contact with the opposite side of the obstacle. Unless it has the [[-Core Rulebook/3-Advanced Rules/1-Special Rules/Universal Special Rules#Fly (X)\|Fly (X)]] [[-Core Rulebook/3-Advanced Rules/1-Special Rules/What Are Special Rules？\|special rule]], a unit that charges an enemy behind a defended obstacle makes a disordered charge.

**[[-Core Rulebook/4-Battlefield Terrain/Categories Of Terrain#^69ac47\|High Linear Obstacles]]:** For the purposes of combat, high linear obstacles (i.e., 2" high or more) are treated as impassable terrain.
#### [[-Core Rulebook/4-Battlefield Terrain/Categories Of Terrain#Battlefield Decoration\|Battlefield Decoration]]
Some terrain features are so small that they cannot reasonably be expected to interfere with combat. Very small decorative elements of terrain less than 2" across, such as a signpost or well, should be ignored as if they were not there – units can freely fight around them. Should the presence of such a feature ever interfere with the positioning of units, simply make note of its position and move it aside, replacing it when it is convenient to do so.
